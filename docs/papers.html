The Nearest-the-Pin Parimutuel

Peter Cotton · working draft v0.1 · full draft · implementation

A parimutuel pool whose outcomes form a continuum. Each participant submits a predictive density (a cloud of Monte-Carlo samples); when the point $z$ is revealed, the pot is split in proportion to the density each placed at $z$, relative to the crowd, a nearest-the-pin reward. It is the engine behind monteprediction.com.

$\Delta W_i = S\,\dfrac{s_i\,q_i(z)}{\sum_j s_j\,q_j(z)} - s_i, \qquad s_i = b\,W_i$

Three claims, all implemented and unit-tested:

  • Self-funding & truthful. The split is zero-sum (the operator bears no risk), and for a log-wealth (Kelly) maximiser the unique optimal report is one's true density, the continuous shadow of "bet your beliefs", with growth governed by the logarithmic score against the crowd. Caveat (v0.2): this is a statement about the density; when the density is built from a sample cloud by KDE, the optimal cloud is the belief deconvolved by the bandwidth — truthful clouds need the jittered pin of the companion paper below.
  • The projection version. In high dimensions the joint density is hard to estimate, so score the cloud through random one-dimensional projections. By the energy-distance projection identity $$\mathrm{ES}(P,y)=c_d^{-1}\,\mathbb{E}_u\big[\mathrm{CRPS}(P_u,\langle u,y\rangle)\big], \qquad c_d=\tfrac{\Gamma(d/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((d+1)/2)},$$ the average sliced CRPS is the multivariate energy score, a strictly proper score built from cheap 1-D evaluations. This is the projection version used for monteprediction's eleven-dimensional clouds, and it places the mechanism inside the random-projections literature (Johnson–Lindenstrauss, sliced Wasserstein, sliced score matching).
  • Two routes to high-dimensional scoring. Turning a joint forecast into a number at $z$ when $d$ is large can go via a structured density (the Schur pseudo-likelihood / Vecchia factorisation, Cotton 2024–25) or via random projection (the sliced energy score). The nearest-the-pin pool runs with either; a conjectured Schur-damped projection score interpolates between them with a single reliability dial $\gamma$.

Read the full working draft for the derivations, the truthfulness argument, the literature links, and the open questions.

Scoring Point-Cloud Distributional Submissions

Peter Cotton · working draft v0.1 · full draft · implementation · prior-art audit

Contests that accept a cloud of samples, smooth it into a KDE, and log-score it at the raw outcome are improper: the optimal cloud is the deconvolution of your belief by the kernel — for a Gaussian belief $N(\mu,\tau^2)$ and bandwidth $h$, submit $N(\mu,\tau^2-h^2)$, shaving exactly the bandwidth off your variance. The repair is one line: if you smooth the forecasts, smooth the outcome too — jitter the pin by the same kernel. Strict properness follows because Gaussian convolution is injective (its characteristic function never vanishes), closing a gap Bröcker & Smith (2007) flagged and left open. Run the proper rung at every smoothing scale and de Bruijn's identity splits the pot: rung differences pay the Fisher divergence (shape, normalisation-free), the coarse rung pays between-mode mass, the total is the log score — the heat-ladder pool.

$\mathrm{KL}(p^*\Vert\rho) = \underbrace{\mathrm{KL}(p^*_T\Vert\rho_T)}_{\text{mode mass}} + \tfrac12\!\int_0^T\! \underbrace{D_F(p^*_t\Vert\rho_t)}_{\text{shape (Hyvärinen)}}\,dt$

Try it: how much should we jitter?

Truth is $N(0,1)$. The mechanism smooths your cloud with KDE bandwidth $h$, and jitters the pin with s.d. $j$. The curves are exact expected scores (closed forms, parity-tested against the Python library), and the whole story is one formula: the score peaks at $v^\ast = 1 + j^2 - h^2$. With $j=0$ (red, no jitter) the mechanism pays you to shave $h^2$ off your variance; with $j>h$ it pays you to pad. Truth-telling sits at $v^\ast=1$ iff $j=h$: jitter exactly as much as you smooth — the jitter is not a tuning knob, it is pinned to the bandwidth. Slide $j$ and watch the green peak cross the truth line.

■ raw score ($j=0$): peak at $v=1-h^2$   ■ jittered-pin score: peak at $v = 1 + j^2 - h^2$   │ truth $v=1$

The draft proves the three theorems (the deconvolution incentive, strict properness of the mollified score via a Wiener-flavoured injectivity lemma, and the heat-ladder decomposition), and records a historical note: the microprediction platform jittered submissions and ground truth from its launch — merely intuitive at the time, a theorem now.

Composition & the algebra of mechanisms

Peter Cotton · research note · full note · runnable demo

The mechanisms in this repository are not a flat list; they compose. Borrowing the operational discipline of microprediction/skaters, every stage speaks one object type (a distributional belief) and threads its own state; the substitution that turns a skater into a market is that a market threads wealth. A small operator set, sequentialise, pool, conjugate, ensemble, residual, then closes over the mechanisms, all generated by convex functions under Legendre duality.

The note states the classic results with proof sketches. The keystone is Savage's characterisation: a regular scoring rule is strictly proper iff it is built from a strictly convex "generalised entropy" $G(p)=S(p;p)$ via a supporting hyperplane,

$S(p,i)=G(p)+\langle\, G'(p),\; e_i-p\,\rangle,\qquad G'(p)\in\partial G(p),$

so that the elicitation regret is exactly the Bregman divergence of $G$, non-negative by convexity:

$S(q;q)-S(p;q)=G(q)-G(p)-\langle\, G'(p),\; q-p\,\rangle = D_G(q,p)\ \ge\ 0.$

Hanson's market maker is then the Legendre conjugate of that generator, $C=R^{\ast}$ with prices $\nabla C$ and worst-case loss equal to the range of $R$ ($b\log n$ for LMSR); the CFMM is the same potential read in dual coordinates; and the probability-integral transform $z=\Phi^{-1}\!\big(F(x)\big)\sim N(0,1)$ is the warrant for a calibration critic. The worked pipeline, an elicitation market feeding that critic, with wealth as the proper-scoring gradient, is the z-stream loop of the microprediction vision (Cotton, Microprediction, MIT Press, 2022), where z-streams ran on a live platform.
