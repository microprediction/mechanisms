# Composition and the Algebra of Mechanisms

### One object type, a small operator set, and convex duality underneath

Peter Cotton · *Working draft v0.1* · 2026

---

## Abstract

Scoring rules, market makers, parimutuels, and opinion pools are usually
presented as a list. This note is about how they compose. Every stage consumes
and emits one object type, a distributional belief; threads wealth as its
state; and is generated by a convex function, with Legendre duality as the
central involution. Savage's characterisation of proper scoring rules,
Hanson's sequentialisation of a score into a market maker, and the
cost-function/CFMM duality are stated with proof sketches, and a small
operator set (sequentialise, pool, conjugate, ensemble, residual) closes over
the catalogue. A worked pipeline chains an elicitation market into a
calibration critic through the probability integral transform, with wealth as
the proper-scoring gradient, and converges numerically. Properness under
composition is settled by known injectivity theorems; conservation across
stages and residual markets are left open.

---

## 1. One object type: a distributional belief

The operational discipline is borrowed from the
[skaters](https://github.com/microprediction/skaters) time-series interface
[@cotton_skaters], a dependency-free successor to
[timemachines](https://github.com/microprediction/timemachines)
[@cotton_timemachines]. A skater has the contract

```python
dists, state = f(y, state)        # dists : list[Dist] for horizons 1..k
```

where every payload is a `Dist`, a weighted Gaussian mixture exposing
`.mean`, `.std`, `.quantile`, `.logpdf`, `.cdf`. Transforms, ensembles, and
search all consume and emit that same type. That closure is the whole reason
a handful of operators suffice.

Every mechanism in the catalogue already speaks the same language:

| Mechanism | As a map on distributions |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| scoring rule | `(Dist, outcome) -> reward` |
| LMSR / cost-function maker | state `-> Dist` (prices are implied probabilities) |
| parimutuel | stakes `-> Dist` (pool fractions are implied probabilities) |
| opinion pool | `[Dist] -> Dist` |
| PIT / calibration | `(Dist F, x) -> z = ` $\Phi^{-1}(F(x))$ |

So the mechanisms are already a category whose objects are distributional
beliefs. What the skater discipline supplies is the operational signature that
turns them into composable pipeline stages, with wealth as the threaded state:

```python
payouts, aggregate, wealth = M(reports, wealth, outcome)
#   reports   : list[Dist]   participants' beliefs      (skater's y)
#   wealth    : state        incentive-bearing          (skater's state)
#   aggregate : Dist         price / implied prob       (skater's dists)
```

A market maker *is* a skater whose state is the inventory vector $\mathbf q$
and whose emitted `Dist` is the price. Hanson's construction is exactly the
skater move: sequentialising a proper scoring rule over a wealth state turns
it into a market maker (Theorem 2).

## 2. The operators

Skaters expose a small operator set; each lifts to a relationship between
mechanisms.

**Conjugation.** `conjugate(skater, transform)` runs a model in a transformed
coordinate and maps back (`difference`, `standardize`, `ema`; all invertible).
It appears twice in the mechanism world:

- *Statistical.* "One market determines a transformation, another tests
  whether it is uniform" is literally conjugation. Market A elicits a
  predictive CDF $F$; that $F$ is the transform; the z-stream
  $z = \Phi^{-1}(F(x))$ is the conjugated coordinate; Market B trades against
  the null that $z$ is iid $N(0,1)$.
- *Convex-analytic.* The cost-function/CFMM duality is the Legendre conjugate
  of the potential (Proposition 3): the trading-function market is the
  cost-function market viewed in dual coordinates.

**Ensemble.** The precision-weighted skater ensemble ($\propto 1/\mathrm{MSE}$)
is an inverse-variance linear opinion pool; the Bayesian ensemble
(log-likelihood with a complexity penalty) is a logarithmic opinion pool
[@genest1986combining]. The nearest-the-pin pool of the companion paper is a
wealth-weighted ensemble whose mixing weights are themselves a market, updated
by realised score.

**Residual chaining.** One stage models what the previous stage got wrong: a
correction (boosting) market on the residual stream. This operator is named
here and analysed as an open direction in the companion research note on
conservation and boosting markets.

**Sequentialise.** The Hanson functor, score $\to$ market maker, with wealth
as state (Theorem 2).

**Pool.** The batch functor, score $\to$ parimutuel: elicitation in one shot
rather than sequentially.

**Spec / grammar.** Skaters serialise a pipeline to data and search over it.
The mechanism analogue is a market over pipelines: the architecture itself
becomes the thing being elicited.

## 3. The generator: convex functions under Legendre duality

Underneath the operators is a single generating object: convex functions, with
Legendre duality as the central involution.

- A convex $G$ generates a strictly proper scoring rule; the induced
  divergence is $G$'s Bregman divergence [@savage1971elicitation;
  @banerjee2005clustering].
- A convex potential $C$ generates a no-arbitrage cost-function market maker
  with prices $\nabla C$ [@abernethy2013efficient].
- The conjugate $C^*$ generates the trading function of a CFMM
  [@frongillo2024axiomatic].

Operations on convex functions then become operations on mechanisms:

| Operation on the potential | Effect on the mechanism |
|---------------------------|--------------------------|
| Legendre conjugation $C \mapsto C^*$ | cost-function maker $\mapsto$ CFMM (the dual reading) |
| scaling $C \mapsto bC$ | the liquidity knob $b$ (depth vs subsidy) |
| inf-convolution $C_1 \mathbin{\square} C_2$ | merging two liquidity sources into one book [@rockafellar1970convex; @moreau1965proximite; @bhaskara2023general] |
| a state monad over wealth | static elicitation $\mapsto$ dynamic, competitive market |

The inf-convolution row is also the risk-sharing literature's composition law:
the market aggregate of several agents' convex risk measures is their infimal
convolution, with the Pareto allocation as minimiser and the common
subgradient as the clearing price [@barrieu2005inf; @jouini2008optimal].

This is the sense in which there is an algebra: objects are distributions; the
generators are convex potentials; the morphisms are sequentialise, pool,
conjugate, ensemble, and residual; and composition closes because every stage
emits the same `Dist` the next stage consumes.

## 4. The classic results, stated

The claim "convex functions generate the mechanisms" is a chain of theorems.
Conventions here are reward-form (higher is better); the repository's
implementations use the loss form, i.e. the negation. Outcomes lie in
$\{1,\dots,n\}$, a report is $p$ in the simplex $\Delta$, and the expected
score of reporting $p$ when one believes $q$ is
$S(p;q)=\sum_i q_i\, S(p,i)$. A scoring rule is *proper* if truthful reporting
is optimal, $S(q;q)\ge S(p;q)$ for all $p,q$, and *strictly proper* if
equality forces $p=q$.

**Theorem 1 (characterisation of proper scoring rules;
@savage1971elicitation, @mccarthy1956measures).** *A regular scoring rule $S$
is proper iff there is a convex function $G:\Delta\to\mathbb{R}$ with*

$$S(p,i) \;=\; G(p) + \langle\, G'(p),\; e_i - p\,\rangle,
\qquad G'(p)\in\partial G(p),$$

*where $e_i$ is the $i$-th unit vector. It is strictly proper iff $G$ is
strictly convex, and then $G(p)=S(p;p)$ is the expected score of a truthful
forecaster (the generalised entropy) and $G'(p)$ is its gradient.*

**Proof sketch.** ($\Leftarrow$) Because $\sum_i q_i(e_i-p)=q-p$, the expected
score collapses to an affine function of the belief,
$S(p;q)=G(p)+\langle G'(p),\,q-p\rangle$. Hence

$$S(q;q)-S(p;q) \;=\; G(q)-G(p)-\langle G'(p),\,q-p\rangle \;=\; D_G(q,p)\ \ge\ 0,$$

the Bregman divergence of $G$, non-negative by the supporting-hyperplane
inequality and zero only at $q=p$ when $G$ is strictly convex.
($\Rightarrow$) Define $G(p):=S(p;p)$. Properness says $G(q)=\max_p S(p;q)$,
a pointwise maximum of functions affine in $q$; a pointwise sup of affine
functions is convex, so $G$ is convex. The maximiser at $q=p$ exhibits
$S(p,\cdot)$ as a supporting hyperplane of $G$ at $p$, which is the displayed
formula with $G'(p)\in\partial G(p)$. Uniqueness of the maximiser is
equivalent to strict convexity. $\blacksquare$

The content of Theorem 1 is the dictionary *proper scoring rule
$\leftrightarrow$ convex function $\leftrightarrow$ Bregman divergence*
[@gneiting2007strictly; @banerjee2005optimality]. The three classics are three
choices of $G$:

| Score | Generator $G(p)=S(p;p)$ | Bregman divergence $D_G(q,p)$ |
|-------|--------------------------|-------------------------------|
| logarithmic | $\sum_i p_i\log p_i$ (negative entropy) | $\mathrm{KL}(q\Vert p)$ |
| Brier (quadratic) | $\lVert p\rVert_2^2$ | $\lVert q-p\rVert_2^2$ |
| spherical | $\lVert p\rVert_2$ | angular separation of $q,p$ |

**Theorem 2 (scoring rule $\Rightarrow$ market maker; @hanson2007logarithmic,
@abernethy2013efficient).** *Let $R=G$, the convex generator of Theorem 1 read
as a regulariser, and define the cost function as its Legendre–Fenchel
conjugate*

$$C(\mathbf q)=\sup_{p\in\Delta}\big(\langle p,\mathbf q\rangle - R(p)\big),
\qquad \text{prices } \ \nabla C(\mathbf q)=\arg\max_{p}(\cdot)\in\Delta .$$

*Then a trader moving inventory $\mathbf q\to\mathbf q'$ pays
$C(\mathbf q')-C(\mathbf q)$, prices are a valid probability vector, the
market is arbitrage-free because $C$ is convex, and the market maker's
worst-case loss is the range of $R$,*

$$\text{worst-case loss}=\sup_p R(p)-\inf_p R(p).$$

*Taking $R(p)=b\sum_i p_i\log p_i$, $b$ times the log-score generator, gives
$C(\mathbf q)=b\log\sum_i e^{q_i/b}$, Hanson's LMSR, with worst-case loss
$b\log n$.*

**Proof sketch.** Conjugate duality gives $\nabla C(\mathbf q)=\arg\max_p$,
which lies in $\Delta$, and $\langle \nabla C,\mathbf 1\rangle=1$ by the
simplex constraint, so prices are probabilities. Telescoping costs over any
trade sequence returning to $\mathbf q$ sum to zero, ruling out arbitrage. The
maker's loss after the market settles on outcome $i$ is bounded by how far $R$
can move between its extremes, $\sup R-\inf R$; for $b$ times negentropy on
$\Delta$ that range is $b\log n$. The conjugacy $C=R^{*}$ is the formal
content of "the log score, run sequentially, is LMSR." $\blacksquare$

**Proposition 3 (cost-function maker $\leftrightarrow$ CFMM is Legendre
duality; @frongillo2024axiomatic).** *A constant-function market maker holds
reserves and accepts trades preserving a concave trading function $\varphi$; a
cost-function maker prices via convex $C$. The two are the same object read
through $C\leftrightarrow C^{*}$: the reachable reserve set of the CFMM is the
(sub)gradient image of $C$, and the price the CFMM quotes equals $\nabla C$ at
the corresponding inventory.*

**Proposition 4 (the probability integral transform;
@rosenblatt1952remarks, @dawid1984prequential).** *If $X$ has continuous CDF
$F$ then $U=F(X)\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$, and hence
$z=\Phi^{-1}(U)\sim N(0,1)$.*

**Proof.** $\Pr(F(X)\le u)=\Pr(X\le F^{-1}(u))=F(F^{-1}(u))=u$. $\blacksquare$

Proposition 4 is the formal warrant for §5: a correctly elicited predictive
distribution makes the z-stream standard normal, so any detectable departure
is exactly a miscalibration a critic can be paid to find. (For discrete
forecasts one uses the randomised or mid-PIT; the demo uses the latter.)

## 5. Worked composition: an elicitation market feeding a calibration critic

The cleanest non-trivial pipeline chains two mechanisms through a feedback
loop (runnable:
[`examples/sim_pipeline.py`](../examples/sim_pipeline.py)):

1. *Elicitation market (the transformation).* A pool of forecasters each
   report a predictive distribution; the wealth-weighted linear opinion pool
   is a single predictive distribution $F_t$. $F_t$ is the transformation, the
   coordinate change that should turn outcomes into noise.
2. *Calibration critic (test for uniformity).* Apply the probability integral
   transform $u_t = F_t(x_t)$; if $F_t$ is right the $u_t$ are Uniform(0,1)
   and $z_t = \Phi^{-1}(u_t)$ is standard normal. The critic measures how far
   the z-stream departs from uniform; its detectable edge is exactly the
   aggregate's miscalibration.
3. *Feedback.* Each forecaster's wealth is updated by its log score, the
   proper-scoring gradient, so wealth flows to the calibrated reports, $F_t$
   sharpens toward the truth, and the critic's edge collapses.

This is a market-native GAN: a generator (the transform market) against a
critic (the uniformity market), with a proper score as the loss and wealth as
the gradient. It is the z-stream mechanism of the microprediction
vision, and z-streams ran on a live forecasting platform
[@cotton2022microprediction].

The demo makes the loop quantitative. Five forecasters (two calibrated, plus
a bull, a bear, and an overconfident report) against an iid $N(0,1)$ stream:

- z-stream uniformity error (total-variation distance from uniform):
  equal-weighted aggregate $0.165$, converged aggregate $0.068$;
- bias (mean $z$) stays near zero throughout: the symmetric bull and bear
  cancel, so the mean never reveals the problem. It is the shape of the PIT
  that is wrong and then fixed, which is why the critic tests uniformity of
  the whole z-stream rather than a moment or two;
- wealth on the two calibrated reports climbs $0.41 \to 1.00$ as the loop
  runs.

## 6. Composition as engineering, and what remains open

Read this way, market and scoring mechanisms are composable pipeline stages
with a property ordinary pipeline stages lack: each stage carries its own
incentive, so the pipeline is self-correcting. One market picks a
transformation; a second market is paid to find structure the first one left
behind; wealth, not a hand-tuned loss, routes credit between them. A
forecasting stack, a feature pipeline, or a data-quality monitor can each be
posed as a chain of elicitation and test markets glued by the PIT.

Three questions attach to the operator set.

1. *Properness under composition.* Settled in the transformation literature:
   properness is preserved under any transformation of forecast and outcome,
   and strict properness iff the transform is injective
   [@allen2023transformed, Prop. 4; @pic2025proper, Prop. 1]. The PIT is the
   injective case; a learned, non-invertible transform loses strictness. The
   Markov-kernel (stochastic-channel) extension is Theorem 2 of the companion
   point-cloud paper.
2. *Incentive compatibility of the chain.* Each market is individually
   truthful; the composite can admit strategic play across stages (cf. the
   equilibrium analysis of the pot split in §2 of the companion paper).
3. *Conservation and residual markets.* Wealth is the threaded state; a
   well-formed pipeline should conserve the elicited *edge*, not just wealth,
   across stage seams, and a residual (boosting) chain of markets remains to
   be constructed. Both are set out with calibrated novelty verdicts in the
   companion research note on conservation and boosting.
